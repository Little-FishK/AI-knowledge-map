How should I define and consistently use project-specific names, abbreviations and jargon?

简体中文

A015 · A Expressing intent · Aligning words and meanings

Key answerFull explanationReferences

Key answer

Supply definitions with custom names and full names with abbreviations; explain distinctions between easily confused terms. “Use our previous terminology” does not supply definitions absent from the current context.

Full explanation

WinoDict tested contextual understanding of unfamiliar words with GPT-3 and PaLM. Definitions helped but performance remained below that for familiar words; giving familiar words new meanings could also suffer interference from their old meanings. Check for conflicts rather than assuming a new abbreviation is immediately mastered. Paper

Maintain a short glossary with four elements: primary name, meaning, acceptable aliases and similar terms that must remain distinct. For example:

“Complete”: the text passed review and links passed checking; a finished first draft does not qualify. “Draft”: text exists but has not passed those checks. “Completion rate”: completed items divided by planned items in this round. Use these names in outputs; determine whether different labels in source material refer to the same thing.

For prompts processing several documents, put this in a separate terminology section rather than mixing it into the material being analyzed. Separating instructions, background and materials also follows Anthropic's structural guidance. Official guide

When a definition changes, state that the old one is obsolete. If only the name changes, say the meaning remains unchanged. This organization aids execution and checking; no universal experimental improvement percentage is established.

References

1. Eisenschlos et al. (2023), WinoDict: Probing language models for in-context word acquisition, EACL

2. Anthropic, Prompting best practices, official guide
